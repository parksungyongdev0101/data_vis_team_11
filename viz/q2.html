Who Is Gaining an Edge — and Who Is Falling Behind?

Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2023–2025 · GitHub Innovation Graph · Team 11
How is AI transforming developers' work, compensation, and careers?
01 · The Surge

AI Went From Novelty to Default

First, the setup. In just three years AI tools went from a minority habit to near-universal among developers. Each square is 1 of every 100 developers — press Play to watch the grid fill from 44 to 79.

02 · The Mood

The Honeymoon Is Over

Everyone is adopting AI — usage climbed from 44% to 79% in three years. So why is the mood souring? Favorability is collapsing and distrust now outweighs trust.

03 · The Twist

Do AI users earn more? In most countries — and the gap is growing

Everyone is adopting AI — but does everyone benefit? Each bubble is a country: above the line, AI users out-earn non-users. Press Play to watch 2023→2025 — most bubbles rise above parity, even as the global pooled number stays negative (the geography illusion).

04 · The Mechanism

The Catch-up Bet

Why the illusion? Lower-income economies adopt AI hardest. Ukraine sits at 61% daily use, the US at 40%. AI is a leapfrog lever — which drags the global median for "AI users" down.

05 · The Fear

The Exposure Paradox

The more deeply you use AI, the more it threatens you (left). Yet within one country, threat perception has no relationship with actual pay (right). Fear tracks exposure, not economics.

06 · The Boundary

The Trust Boundary

Developers hand AI the keyboard for exploration — but keep humans on the levers that touch production. There's a sharp cliff between the two.

07 · The Real Junior Risk

The Atrophy Gradient

The genuine risk to juniors isn't lower pay (a statistical mirage) — it's developmental. 1 in 3 juniors feel AI is eroding their own problem-solving skills, versus 1 in 12 of the most senior.

08 · The Divide

The Adoption Divide

And none of this is evenly distributed. "AI-first" (fintech, startups, AI/ML engineers, the young) pulls away from "AI-cautious" (government, big corp, academia, 55+).
